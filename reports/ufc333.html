What this card tells us

This card does not validate the historical filters: their preferred high-confidence and opposing-pick positions performed poorly, while a large winner lay outside them. It illustrates the uncertainty behind the earlier attractive backtests. One card also cannot establish that the underlying confidence scale is useless or that backing all picks is the better long-run rule. The shrinkage versions protected capital mainly by reducing or eliminating participation.

Calculation and limits

  • Apply previously defined intervals and six probability models without selecting new rules.
  • Refit only on earlier settled fights, including earlier fights on this card. Retention scores earlier out-of-fit forecasts.
  • Use the spreadsheet YES asks. Buy NO at 1 minus same-candle YES bid. All 13 asks and outcomes match retrieved feeds.
  • Each fight settles before the next stake. Reset card-start bankroll to one.
  • Same continuous standard-taker fee approximation: ask + 0.07*ask*(1-ask). Fractional contracts; actual contract and fee rounding omitted.
  • Quotes were recorded before the card, not at each sequential entry. Executability and liquidity are not established.
  • The workbook was supplied with results; pre-fight existence of its picks is not independently certified. This is a retrospective application of pre-existing rules.
  • No new statistical edge claim is inferred from one card. No original or audited workbook is modified.

The first forecast uses 185 earlier decisive results. The model then learns from each settled fight, including fights on which it did not bet. Kelly uses f = max(0, (p − all-in cost)/(1 − all-in cost)), multiplied by the selected Kelly fraction and limited by the selected cap.

These are conditional results at the supplied pre-card prices. Holding those prices fixed while recycling bankroll does not demonstrate that the same quotes would have remained available before every later fight.

The continuous fee approximation follows the standard taker expression in the Kalshi fee schedule. It omits contract granularity, per-order cent rounding and slippage. No new interval search, stake fraction optimization or statistical-significance claim was introduced for this card.
