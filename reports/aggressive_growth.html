What is being compared

Twenty single-rule and combined portfolios, three probability estimators, two price samples, two execution conventions, and four sizing choices produce 960 scenarios. The 876 feasible trajectories each include all 187 recorded rows plus the initial bankroll. The candidates come from the earlier analyses. These results identify winners within this comparison, not a mathematical optimum over every conceivable strategy.

Each portfolio uses one bankroll, at most one position per fight, and a common Kelly fraction across its constituent categories. The probability-model family is also common across constituents. All primary tables and plots refit probabilities after earlier fights settle and reinvest the updated bankroll. Each stake is a fraction of the bankroll immediately before that fight. There is no requirement to finance every selected fight on a card simultaneously.

Prices are Kalshi purchase asks with modeled taker fees; missing prices remain reconstructed. For an opposite-side purchase, NO ask is one minus the YES bid, preserving the spread under Kalshi's documented reciprocal pricing. Intervals described for fades refer to the original pick's YES ask. These are scalable bankroll ratios, not evidence that large dollar amounts could have been filled.

The sequential scenario assumes each result settles in time to fund the next fight and that the saved or reconstructed price remains obtainable when that next bet is placed. Actual execution depth and intra-card quote changes are unavailable. The primary comparison accepts the requested settlement assumption; the historical prices remain proxies for execution.

What a 27.5% half-Kelly stake means

It means risking 27.5% on one fight. Half Kelly halves the model's calculated optimal bankroll fraction; it does not impose a small fixed percentage. For a binary contract with all-in cost c and estimated win probability p, full Kelly is max(0, (p − c) / (1 − c)). Multiply that fraction by the chosen Kelly multiplier and the current bankroll to obtain the all-in stake.

At the largest half-Kelly position in the full-history pooled combination, the ask is 77¢, modeled all-in cost is 78.2397¢, and the estimated win probability is 90.2151%. That produces 55.0334% full Kelly and 27.5167% half Kelly. With $1,000 immediately before this fight, the illustrative all-in stake is $275.17. A loss leaves $724.83; a win leaves $1,076.53 under the scalable fee approximation. The next stake is calculated from whichever new balance results and the next fight's updated estimate. The 90.2% is a model forecast, not an independently established win probability.

For win/loss settlement, the recursion is B_next = B × (1 − f + f × y / c), where f is the all-in bankroll fraction, y = 1 for a win and y = 0 for a loss. We reset the bankroll base after each fight while retaining earlier outcomes for probability estimation. Current bankroll and the model's current estimates provide the state for the next decision.

My judgment

For the question “what would have made the most money in this workbook?”, the narrow undefeated pair with category-specific probability estimates is the answer among the compared portfolios. Its sequential quarter-Kelly version grows 18.615× and full Kelly reaches 6,595.711×. It deserves to be shown, with the explicit explanation that selected winning streaks and increasingly confident local estimates drive the result.

For an aggressive strategy worth testing on subsequent picks, the high-confidence backing core plus confidence-1 favorite fades remains an interpretable framework. Quarter, half and full Kelly are fundable under the requested timing. The pooled full-history result favors full Kelly, but its drawdown exceeds 50%, and observed-only full Kelly ends below half Kelly with a 79.1% drawdown. Half Kelly is an aggressive comparison point, not a demonstrated optimal future fraction. The simpler ≥35¢ core is close to the optimized 35–81¢ version.

I would treat confidence-2 underdogs and the two narrow winning categories as separately tracked hypotheses before allowing their small local samples to dominate stake estimates. Use the pooled probability estimate as the main sizing comparison, update only after settlement, price the actual side including fees, and do not stack overlapping categories. Search-adjusted evidence from the earlier reports still does not establish a profitable strategy. This new portfolio comparison adds hindsight choices and does not strengthen that statistical confidence.
