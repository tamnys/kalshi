What would have grown the bankroll aggressively?

October 19, 2026 · Retrospective comparison of the categories already discovered. No new interval mining.

For a broader aggressive framework, combine backing confidence 4–5 at 35–81¢ with opposing confidence-1 favorites. With pooled probabilities and half Kelly, that combination grows the bankroll to 2.225× with 33.3% maximum drawdown, reserving cash before each card and settling every fight. The largest hindsight gains come from the two narrow undefeated categories: category-based probability estimates produce 15.253× at quarter Kelly or 57.215× at the largest cash-feasible common fraction, 40.90% of Kelly. Those extreme results are heavily conditioned on finding two winning streaks after seeing outcomes.

What is being compared

Twenty single-rule and combined portfolios, three probability estimators, two price samples, two execution conventions, and four sizing choices produce 960 scenarios. The 876 feasible trajectories each include all 187 recorded rows plus the initial bankroll. The candidates come from the earlier analyses. These results identify winners within this comparison, not a mathematical optimum over every conceivable strategy.

Each portfolio uses one bankroll, at most one position per fight, and a common Kelly fraction across its constituent categories. The probability-model family is also common across constituents. All primary tables reserve cash before each card; odds and forecasts are fixed at that time, while equity updates after each fight. The alternative sequential convention refits and reinvests between fights but assumes the pre-card quotes remain obtainable later. Actual quote depth and settlement timing are unavailable.

Prices are Kalshi purchase asks with modeled taker fees; missing prices remain reconstructed. For an opposite-side purchase, NO ask is one minus the YES bid, preserving the spread under Kalshi's documented reciprocal pricing. Intervals described for fades refer to the original pick's YES ask. These are scalable bankroll ratios, not evidence that large dollar amounts could have been filled.

Choice 1: a broader aggressive combination

The two components address different signals: confidence-4–5 picks perform better after excluding cheap prices, while confidence-1 favorites have been unusually poor picks and therefore profitable to oppose. They never bet the same fight. Combining them improves the historical growth without adding another overlapping stake on an existing position.

The simplest version uses confidence 4–5 at any ask ≥35¢ and fades confidence-1 favorites. At half Kelly it returns 2.103× with 33.3% drawdown. Using the retrospectively stronger upper boundary of 81¢ improves terminal wealth to 2.225×, also with 33.3% drawdown. The small gain from that upper cut is less compelling than the gain from adding the confidence-1 fades.

That retrospectively optimal fraction grows to 2.666× with 40.7% drawdown. It is an observed financing boundary, not a forward risk recommendation. Full Kelly cannot fund this portfolio at the pre-card prices without borrowing or changing allocations.

The quarter-to-half increase raises terminal growth from +54.6% to +122.5%, while historical drawdown rises from 18.5% to 33.3%. Without a user-selected drawdown preference, no one of these is universally optimal. Choice 2: concentrate on the two undefeated categories

  1. Back confidence 3–5 at YES asks from 35¢ to below 40¢: eight wins from eight qualifying fights.
  2. Oppose confidence-1 picks whose original YES ask was 25–70¢: six wins from six qualifying fights; actual NO asks ranged from 32 to 76¢.

The categories are disjoint. A category-offset model learns each category's excess win probability from earlier category outcomes only. Because every later qualifying result was a win, its estimated advantage and bankroll both grow. This converts a small winning streak into an enormous compounding outcome.

The strongest cash-feasible common fraction in this narrow combination is 40.9016% of Kelly. It gives 57.215× terminal wealth, taking 12 positions after initial category learning, with a largest individual allocation of 36.4%. On the most heavily allocated card it commits the entire bankroll across the selected fights. Half Kelly would require 122.2% of available card cash, so its pre-card result is infeasible. Quarter Kelly uses up to 61.1% of card cash and produces 15.253×.

With the pooled model, the same categories give 2.727× at half Kelly and 6.327× at full Kelly, both cash-feasible. Thus much of the extreme result comes from the probability estimator believing the small category's undefeated history, not simply from choosing a larger fraction of the same estimate.

Under the sequential reinvestment assumption, category-based half Kelly yields 187.927× and full Kelly yields 6,595.711×. Those numbers are mathematically consistent with twelve winning bets carrying large bankroll allocations, but should not be interpreted as achievable historical trading results: the simulator reuses earlier pre-card quotes, assumes scalable fills and prompt availability of proceeds, and selects the categories after observing the streaks. The pre-card cash analysis removes one of those assumptions and sharply reduces the headline.

Using only observed quotes for fitting and trading leaves four category-model positions. Quarter Kelly produces 2.029× and full Kelly 9.224×. The much smaller sample cannot establish that the all-history extreme growth rate persists. All eight narrow backing wins and all six narrow fade wins were used in discovering the filters, so forecast fitting on earlier outcomes does not remove the selection bias.

Zero historical drawdown here means no chosen bet lost. It is not a bound on future drawdown. A loss at the largest sequential full-Kelly stake would remove 88.9% of the then-current bankroll; even the cash-feasible pre-card version can lose 36.4% on one position, or potentially all of a fully allocated card. The mathematical growth figures are genuine backtest results, but the probability error that matters most is not measured by their zero-loss paths.

More groups increase both opportunities and overlapping cash needs, and the extra filters are all selected retrospectively.

The narrower confidence-1 fade improves retrospective growth relative to fading all favorites, but it also removes a losing fade just above the 70¢ boundary. The broader favorite rule is the more interpretable hypothesis to test with new data. Blanket confidence-1 fading produces less growth and more drawdown than favorite-only fading in this comparison.

My judgment

For the question “what would have made the most money in this workbook?”, the narrow undefeated pair with category-specific probability estimates is the answer among the compared portfolios. Even its quarter-Kelly, pre-card-funded version grows more than fifteenfold. It deserves to be shown rather than dismissed, but it is an extreme retrospective result.

For an aggressive strategy worth testing on subsequent picks, the high-confidence backing core plus confidence-1 favorite fades is the stronger framework. Half Kelly is an aggressive comparison point supported by its historical doubling, model/source sensitivity, and available cash; it is not a demonstrated optimal fraction. Quarter Kelly offers a lower-drawdown comparison, and the more aggressive cash-bound fraction shows the price of chasing additional growth. The simpler ≥35¢ core is close to the optimized 35–81¢ version.

I would treat confidence-2 underdogs and the two narrow winning categories as separately tracked hypotheses before allowing their small local samples to dominate stake estimates. Use the pooled probability estimate as the main sizing comparison, update only after settlement, price the actual side including fees, and do not stack overlapping categories. Search-adjusted evidence from the earlier reports still does not establish a profitable strategy. This new portfolio comparison adds hindsight choices and does not strengthen that statistical confidence.

Verification and interpretation of the stress numbers

Every combination is checked for duplicate fight exposure. The new single-rule results reconcile with earlier outputs, and independent cash/contract accounting reproduces all feasible trajectories. The continuous fraction optimum is checked using the concavity and first-order conditions of log terminal wealth over the cash-feasible interval. Single-win reversals change one executed winning payout to a loss while holding every forecast stake fraction fixed; they do not refit subsequent probabilities, change interval selection, or estimate a probability of loss. The worst terminal and worst drawdown can come from different reversed fights.
