## 2026-10-19 — assessment of actual edge versus overfitting

The requested outcome is an assessment of what the completed investigation establishes about genuine predictive and trading edge. The evidence is the existing category statistics, search adjustments, earlier-data forecasts, price-source comparisons and sequential sizing results; this assessment requires no new interval search or simulation.

There is enough evidence to retain selective-edge hypotheses, especially high-confidence backing and opposing confidence-1 favorites. There is not enough evidence to claim a validated profitable system, calibrated aggressive Kelly stakes, or the enormous returns of the best historical combinations. A modest real advantage and substantial overfitting of its apparent magnitude can coexist. The data do not let us assign a defensible single probability that the proprietary system has an edge.

The most informative comparisons use equal cash stakes after modeled Kalshi fees, before Kelly amplification:

The 35¢ high-confidence rule remains profitable when any one card is omitted: its minimum such ROI is +19.9%. Its recovered-price subset returns +24.1%. These checks make the result worth pursuing, but reuse the selected observations and do not constitute independent replications. The 35–81¢ alternative raises ROI to +29.5% over 43 fights; the precise upper cutoff has less evidential justification than the broader hypothesis. Both filters have a both-direction search-adjusted p-value of 1.0 in the existing scan. Their positive nominal confidence intervals must not be read as selection-adjusted confidence in the discovered strategy.

The original investigation examined 104,312 overlapping backing/fading intervals against only 185 decisive fights across 15 cards. They are not independent tests; the simulation accounts for searching the correlated interval family under its stated null, with a Bonferroni adjustment across backing and fading. The striking confidence-3–5, 35 to below 40¢ interval won 8/8. Its nominal all-win null probability is about 0.056%, but its search-adjusted p-value is about 0.178 for backing and 0.357 across both directions. No discovered category separates convincingly from chance after the applicable full search correction. These p-values are not probabilities that an edge exists or does not exist. They also do not completely account for all subsequent discretion over models, portfolio combinations, Kelly fractions and caps.

The narrower confidence-1 fade interval has six wins and no losses. That and the eight-win backing sample explain much of the spectacular compounded growth. Repeatedly fitting and staking against an undefeated sample can generate increasingly confident probabilities and very large positions. Resampling these recorded winners cannot reveal how the strategy behaves when a new loss occurs. The confidence-2 and confidence-3 pockets remain exploratory because of small samples, boundary sensitivity and mixed earlier-data performance.

The existing 161-fight earlier-outcome forecast comparison is another restraint on confidence. The unadjusted quote baseline has Brier score 0.2091 and log loss 0.6043; pooled odds-plus-confidence gives 0.2109 and 0.6140, respectively, where lower is better. None of the fitted alternatives clearly improves the baseline, and the uncertainty intervals for log-loss differences include zero. The baseline quote is not a known true probability, and an overall scoring comparison can miss a useful localized trading edge. Nevertheless, this test does not demonstrate that confidence adds dependable information beyond price.

Fitting each probability only from earlier settled fights avoids direct current-outcome leakage. It does not make a retrospectively chosen category or portfolio out of sample. The whole workbook has been available throughout the investigation, and the dated split tests were designed during that investigation. There is no untouched test set. Our additional searches and comparisons increase the opportunity for overfitting even when every cash-flow calculation is correct. This distinction is consistent with the multiple-configuration problem described by [Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado and Zhu](https://scholarworks.wmich.edu/math_pubs/40/).

Only 68 decisive fights have recovered Kalshi quotes; the remaining decisive prices are reconstructed. Observed-only results therefore matter, although those simulations change both training and traded observations. None establishes that the historical pre-card asks were executable at each sequential entry. The source workbook also cannot establish complete prospective pick capture or unchanged proprietary-model versions. These are limitations of the evidence, not allegations about the system.
