Does learning the Kelly fraction from earlier results help?

The new learned-fraction policy chooses a common multiplier in [0,1] that maximizes log growth over strictly earlier opportunities, then uses that multiplier for the next fight. It starts with zero when there is no evidence or the objective is flat. It can learn from a prior observable result even if it skipped the bet. Probability forecasts also continue updating after each settlement. This is an earlier-data rule for sizing; the candidate categories themselves remain retrospective.

What the comparisons support

The simpler aggressive candidate remains confidence-4–5 backing at 35–81¢ plus confidence-1 favorite fades, using one pooled probability model. Quarter and half Kelly show the growth/drawdown tradeoff without pretending that a preferred risk tolerance was supplied. Full Kelly is fundable sequentially and wins on the full price history, but is substantially worse than half Kelly in the recovered-price sensitivity.

The stronger narrow candidate in these comparisons replaces the broad favorite fades with C1 fades at original asks of 25–70¢. It improves both price-sample results and stays profitable across the tested half-Kelly model variants. Its sharp, retrospectively chosen boundary needs genuinely new outcomes before it should dominate confidence in the strategy.

The five-group combination is the more aggressive exploratory portfolio. At pooled half Kelly it gives 5.027× over 49 bets in the full price history and 1.709× over 18 recovered-price bets. The extra groups raise historical growth, but their benefits vary with the source and model. They do not establish a superior future strategy. The undefeated pair remains a separate high-upside hypothesis whose extreme growth should not dictate probability estimates.

Re-estimating the multiplier after every fight is not supported as an automatic improvement by these checks. Maintain one bankroll, update the model only from settled outcomes, price the actual purchased side including fees, and avoid overlapping positions. Kelly's growth objective and a drawdown constraint are distinct, as discussed in Busseti, Ryu and Boyd's risk-constrained Kelly study. The present results show the tradeoffs without inventing a drawdown target or stake cap.

Scope, accounting and reproducibility

The existing audited dataset contains 185 decisive fights, one draw and one cancellation across 15 cards. Chronology follows the audited ESPN fight order. Missing Kalshi prices remain reconstructed; forecasts do not establish executable later-fight quotes or market depth. Opposite-side asks use one minus the YES bid, following Kalshi's reciprocal orderbook pricing. No live bets were placed.

All 600 paths were independently reconstructed through cash spent, contract quantities and settlement payouts. The 480 previously computed scenarios reconcile with earlier outputs. Every learned-fraction decision was checked against strictly earlier returns, and frozen-fraction optimality and resampling distributions were separately checked. Earlier search-adjusted results still do not establish a statistically reliable profitable category; additional sizing comparisons cannot turn the same retrospective sample into independent evidence.
