MMA picks: Kalshi edge and fractional Kelly analysis

Prepared October 18, updated October 19, 2026 (America/New_York). Source: MMA betting – UFC Edmonton Oct 17 2026 – results updated.xlsx. Research, intermediate outputs, and code: tamnys/kalshi.

The data supports a plausible filtered opportunity, but does not establish a reliably profitable strategy. Confidence 4–5 provides a broad reference: 56 bets, 37 wins, and 9.7% net ROI after modeled Kalshi taker fees. The uncertainty interval includes losses. Betting every pick loses 0.9%.

Updated interval analysis: the October 19 follow-up and trajectory viewer tests every positive-return interval over the complete fight sequence. Confidence 4–5 at 35–81¢ returns +29.5% flat-stake ROI; the simpler 35¢-and-above rule returns +25.4%. These are promising prospective-tracking candidates selected retrospectively. Use one calibrated model and one bankroll, and do not stack overlapping rules. The 35–40¢ confidence 3–5 band remains a separate hypothesis, not an 8-for-8 probability estimate.

The follow-up also shows estimation risk: beginning the odds-plus-confidence model as soon as it is identifiable, after three fights, changes broad confidence-4–5 quarter-Kelly growth from +10.2% to −7.2%. The original 24-fight comparison below is retained with its training convention stated explicitly. No stake fraction is established as uniquely optimal.

Further checks: the earlier-data selection and Kelly robustness report tests category selection using only past outcomes, all existing probability models, pre-card allocations for the new filters, and 10,000 whole-card calibration refits. The 35¢-and-above confidence-4–5 rule retains +22.5% quarter-Kelly growth with pre-card allocations, while adaptive category selection and category-specific sizing are unstable. These checks strengthen the case for tracking a fixed candidate, but do not establish future profitability.

Lower confidence and opposing picks: the confidence 1–3 report and interactive trajectories separately test backing each lower level and buying NO against confidence 1 or 2. Opposing confidence-1 favorites is the clearest additional candidate; confidence-2 underdogs and confidence-3 favorite intervals have less stable support.

Aggressive bankroll growth: the retrospective portfolio comparison combines discovered rules in one bankroll, settling each fight before sizing the next bet. It explains individual Kelly stakes, growth, drawdown and probability-model sensitivity; simultaneous pre-card funding is a separate comparison.

The positive fight-by-fight Kelly simulation assumes the recorded pre-card prices remain available when later fights are bet. When probabilities and allocations are frozen before the card instead, the high-confidence quarter-Kelly simulation loses 7.7%. This execution distinction is material.

How much is category discovery inflating the apparent edge?

The search evaluated every contiguous confidence interval and every contiguous interval of observed prices: 52,156 intervals per direction, with no minimum sample-size filter. Both backing and fading were examined, for 104,312 total intervals. Equal-price observations were kept together.

The strongest backing interval is confidence 3–5 at 35¢ to below 40¢: eight wins in eight fights across seven cards, +154.9% net ROI. Under a pre-specified independent net-breakeven hypothesis, the chance of eight wins at these prices is about 0.056%. But the interval was selected after looking at all results. Comparing its statistic with the maximum across the full backing search gives p ≈ 0.178; accounting conservatively for both search directions gives p ≈ 0.357. The apparent certainty therefore does not survive the search.

Six of those eight entry prices are reconstructed; only two are recovered Kalshi asks. Reassigning observations after a common adverse reconstruction error reduces the band to six fights and +29.5% ROI. It remains interesting, but its precise boundaries and observed 100% win rate should not be treated as established properties of the system.

Fading confidence-1 favorites is also interesting: five wins in seven bets, +80.6%. Its model-based positive-edge probability is 97.4%, but its card-cluster interval includes a loss, and the exhaustive search-adjusted result offers no support. Fading all picks loses 8.6%. I would keep this as a secondary research hypothesis, not combine it into the initial strategy.

Historical Kalshi prices and the reconstruction model

Both the live and historical Kalshi APIs were paginated through the KXUFCFIGHT series, yielding 1,570 markets. The oldest indexed opening is May 8, 2025. For each match, the last available ask candle ending at or before the ESPN card start was used; all recovered endpoints coincide with card start. A settled market’s last price was never used as a betting price. Market-side settlements were checked against the corrected outcomes.

The recovered ESPN competition dates identify broadcast segments rather than individual fight starts: every card has only two or three distinct competition timestamps, with multiple bouts sharing each segment time. In the cached records, date, startDate and endDate are identical for each bout. Those fields cannot validate a price immediately before a particular fight. The card-by-card evidence is saved in results/quote_timing_audit.csv; the sequential-price limitation is therefore retained.

69 fight prices are recovered asks (68 decisive fights and the draw); 115 are estimated from the selected fighter’s sportsbook quote; two are estimated from the opponent. There were no remaining rows requiring the workbook’s Kalshi snapshot as a fallback. On the 68 observed-price decisive fights, net flat-stake ROI is 0.4%. Missing historical prices remain estimates, including hypothetical Kalshi prices for fights predating the series.

For an American quote A, first compute sportsbook implied probability q: q = |A|/(|A|+100) for a negative quote; q = 100/(A+100) for a positive quote. The chosen mapping is:

estimated Kalshi ask = logistic(−0.0439227 + 0.9490583 × logit(q))

Its coefficients use 44 independent quote pairs across five cards. Identity conversion, a constant logit shift, and the two-parameter model were compared by leaving out one complete card at a time. The chosen model’s out-of-card mean absolute error is 1.13¢; RMSE is 1.45¢, versus 1.95¢ for direct conversion. Empirical held-out residuals run from −2.72¢ to +2.95¢ between their 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. These are observed error quantiles, not guaranteed prediction bounds.

The 25 recent American odds were derived from Kalshi cents, so they were excluded from fitting to avoid circular validation. The bid mapping is logistic(−0.1249555 + 0.9442620 × logit(q)); buying No costs one minus the Yes bid. Opposite-side spread is therefore retained. Outcome labels are not used anywhere in price-model fitting. Unknown sportsbook timestamps, bookmaker margin, and quote depth still prevent exact reconstruction.

These estimates are conditional mean prices and can have sub-cent precision; they do not imply a historical fill at that precision. A further sensitivity using the direct sportsbook conversion gives −1.8% overall ROI and +8.2% high-confidence ROI. Current taker fees are applied uniformly as an all-Kalshi execution scenario, rather than claiming to reproduce every historical fee regime.

The standard fee approximation is 0.07 × price × (1 − price) per contract. Total cash at risk is c = price + fee, the net win return is 1/c − 1, and a loss returns −1. Actual rounding depends on membership and order fills; the analysis uses scalable continuous stakes. For implementation, use the fee shown on the order ticket. Kalshi fee schedule; rounding rules.

A fight-by-fight fractional Kelly strategy

Use one bankroll for the pooled confidence-4–5 candidates. Before each fight, obtain the executable ask and fee, refit calibration using only previously settled fights, and bet only when the calibrated probability exceeds all-in cost. If multiple category descriptions select the same fight, place one position. Using favorites and underdogs in the same pool does not require splitting bankroll into separate category accounts.

A reproducible starting calibration, fitted to the complete cleaned binary sample, is:

p̂ = logistic(0.0155981 + 0.8998197 × logit(Kalshi ask)
              + 0.2962599 × (confidence − 3))

c = ask + actual per-contract fee
full-Kelly cash fraction = max(0, (p̂ − c) / (1 − c))
fractional stake = bankroll × λ × full-Kelly cash fraction
contracts ≈ fractional stake / c

The fit uses Firth/Jeffreys bias reduction to keep early, sparse estimates finite. This is a fitted starting model, not a certified true probability. Update it using the same method as new settled observations arrive; do not substitute a tiny bucket’s raw win percentage. At confidence 4 and a 40¢ ask, the current fit gives p̂ ≈ 48.7%, c = 41.68¢ and full Kelly ≈ 12.0% of bankroll. A quarter-Kelly illustration is 3.0%. These are conditional sizing examples, not verified future edges.

For a nonzero draw probability, use three outcomes rather than calling a draw a refund: returns are 1/c − 1, −1, and 0.5/c − 1. Maximize the probability-weighted sum of log(1 + f × return). The workbook has only one draw, so it cannot support a precise fight-specific draw model. The binary sizing formula is an approximation; realized draw settlement is included in the accounting. Every backing model skipped the observed draw. One categorical fading model held a position and its draw loss is included.

Fraction choice: you specified no drawdown preference, so the data does not determine a unique “optimal” fractional Kelly. The growth-maximizing fraction in the high-confidence historical simulation is about 0.412; choosing it after observing the outcomes would overfit. I would not use the fitted full-Kelly sizing on this evidence.

Recalculate available bankroll only after settlement; outstanding exposure is not reusable cash. The sequential simulation refits after each fight, as requested. Its prices are nevertheless pre-card snapshots. The separate sensitivity that freezes both forecasts and allocations before the card yields −7.7% at quarter Kelly for the high-confidence pool and −27.6% for all model edges. It has different information timing, and demonstrates that the positive sequential simulation is not proof of a historically executable trading return.

Before increasing any allocation, collect the actual signal timestamp, confidence, both bid/ask quotes, order fee, fill price and quantity, settlement time, and every eligible skipped or placed pick. Preserve the proposed categories and model specification while evaluating those new records. A favorable threshold should not be redefined after every loss. There is no invented minimum number of future bets here; the uncertainty should be recalculated from the accumulating evidence.

Statistical definitions and reproducibility

Flat-stake ROI is the average of y/c − 1. The win-rate percentage alone is insufficient because payouts differ. The aggregate net profit is -1.6346 units over 185 decisive fights; gross profit before fees is 4.1024 units. Including the draw gives net ROI -0.86%.

The frequentist null for each category gives every fight its all-in breakeven win probability, with independent Bernoulli outcomes. The search statistic is sum(return) / sqrt(sum((1 − c)/c)). For each simulated dataset the maximum across all intervals is retained. The two directional searches are combined with a Bonferroni factor of two. This adjustment covers the stated category family; it does not retroactively preregister the broader modeling investigation.

10,000 simulations are used because a Bernoulli Monte Carlo probability then has worst-case standard error 0.005, matching probabilities reported to two decimal places. The seed is derived from the source workbook hash. The quoted 95% intervals are descriptive conventional uncertainty summaries, not a 5% rule for placing bets.

The posterior model sets pᵢ = logistic(logit(askᵢ) + δ), with Jeffreys density proportional to sqrt(sum(pᵢ(1 − pᵢ))). The reported probability integrates posterior mass where the average expected return at the observed prices exceeds zero. It assumes a common shift within the category, independent outcomes and stability of its price mix. A positive observed ROI and a low posterior positive-edge probability can coexist when profits depend heavily on a long-shot win.

Price-error scenarios, leave-one-card-out checks, calendar blocks, influential-winner removal, observed-versus-estimated splits, opposite-side trading, frozen forecasts, sequential forecasts, and stake-fraction comparisons are saved as separate CSVs. No generic classifier with many fighter-level features was added: the supplied data cannot identify such a model reliably. Missing pre-fight timestamps and missing source picks remain unresolvable from the workbook rather than being invented.
