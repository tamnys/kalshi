Earlier-data selection and Kelly robustness

October 19, 2026 · Extension of the interval search and full-trajectory viewer and source audit.

The fixed high-confidence price filters remain plausible opportunities, but their impressive historical returns are not yet a validated adaptive betting system. Confidence 4–5 at 35¢ and above earns +22.5% at quarter Kelly when forecasts and allocations are both fixed before each card. Restricting fitting and trading to recovered Kalshi quotes gives +9.5%, from only six bets. Those rules were still selected using the complete history.

Updated recommendation: retain confidence 4–5 at 35¢ and above as the simplest candidate for a fixed, prospectively recorded trial. The 35–81¢ version is a useful comparison, not an established optimal cutoff. Use a pooled probability model and one position per fight; inspect the market-anchored confidence model as a sizing sensitivity. Do not select a new winning bucket after every result or size a bet from a tiny bucket’s eventual win percentage. The data supports investigating this filter, while leaving substantial uncertainty about future profitability and the appropriate Kelly fraction.

Selecting the rule using earlier results only

The adaptive experiments choose a category after each fight or before each card using only earlier decisive results. They compare the highest training ROI, highest return divided by its null standard deviation, and highest prior quarter-Kelly log growth. The last objective uses the already-defined pooled odds/confidence stakes. Each selected rule is then sized either by that pooled model or by a common offset fitted to earlier results inside the selected category. The rule-selection objective is held fixed when comparing these sizing methods.

Price boundaries are midpoints between adjacent distinct training prices, with outer boundaries 0 and 1. They do not use future prices to define unseen-price membership. Score ties within floating-point accumulation error favor more training observations, then broader coverage, then lexical bounds. There is no minimum sample-size gate. A nonpositive best score produces no rule.

All twelve quarter-Kelly adaptive variants using pooled odds/confidence sizing lose money. With selected-category sizing, the highest-training-ROI rule produces +145.2% growth and 31.0% maximum drawdown when reselected after every fight. That same method loses 49.1% on observed-price-only data. Its card-reselected version grows 69.0% in the reconstructed history and loses 33.1% on observed-only data. The other category-specific selection criteria perform poorly. Positive flat-stake selection returns and negative Kelly returns can coexist because the model sizes or skips different bets.

How uncertain are current Kelly sizes?

10,000 resamples each draw 15 complete cards with replacement and refit the odds-plus-confidence model. This includes parameter re-estimation, unlike the earlier bootstrap that merely reordered already-fitted forecast/return paths. It preserves within-card dependence while conditioning on the available prices, model specification and recorded cards. At confidence 4 and a 40¢ ask, the point estimate is a 48.7% win probability and a 3.0% quarter-Kelly bankroll stake. The central refit range is 33.4–63.8% probability and 0–9.5% stake, with a positive estimated edge in 81.2% of fits. At an 85¢ ask, only 56.7% of refits show a positive edge for confidence 4. Higher confidence increases the fitted probability, but confidence 5 has only seven observations in the original sample and wider parameter uncertainty. Every displayed central 95% stake range includes zero.

The confidence coefficient’s central bootstrap range is approximately −0.022 to +0.639 in log-odds units per confidence level. That is consistent with useful confidence information, but does not settle its magnitude or stability. Full Kelly treats a point estimate as sufficiently accurate for log-growth sizing; these refits show why that assumption is consequential. They do not identify a unique optimal fractional multiplier.

Evidence after adaptive strategy selection

A separate conditional diagnostic uses the bankroll itself as an evidence process. If conditional expected settlement never exceeds all-in cost, and an algorithm chooses its stake from past information without borrowing, expected bankroll cannot increase under that null. The reciprocal of peak bankroll then gives a conservative bound valid across stopping times. Averaging prespecified wealth processes also preserves the bound. Howard et al., time-uniform bounds; Vovk and Wang, e-value combination.

The profitable adaptive quarter-Kelly ROI strategy ends at 2.45× bankroll and peaks at 3.55×, giving an individual inverse-peak bound of approximately 0.281. Across all 72 adaptive fraction/method variants, Bonferroni bounds are 1.000. The equally weighted wealth mixture peaks at 1.59×, giving a bound of 0.627. This diagnostic offers no strong support for selecting the impressive winner. It covers this explicitly enumerated family under its price and timing assumptions; it does not retroactively preregister the wider research process.

What to carry forward

The evidence favors keeping a simple high-confidence price filter stable while collecting new picks, rather than repeatedly optimizing categories. For each eligible fight, obtain the actual ask and fee, update the pooled probability model using settled earlier fights, and calculate the cash fraction from that estimate. Compare fractional stakes against the drawdown tradeoffs, and avoid interpreting the current 3% example as a universal stake. Record all signals and skipped bets, not just fills. Evaluate new data against the fixed rule before deciding whether to revise it.

The absence of an established edge is not evidence that every filtered opportunity is worthless: the high-confidence filters remain positive across models, influence checks, price stress and the quote-time allocation test. The unresolved issues are genuine future replication, signal completeness, timestamp integrity, model drift, and executable prices. Further rearrangement of this same small history cannot resolve those issues.
