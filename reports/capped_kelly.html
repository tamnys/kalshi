What do 5%, 10%, 15% and 20% bet caps cost?

October 19, 2026 · 540 scenarios · six leading portfolios · quarter, half and full Kelly · two price samples · three probability models

The caps sharply reduce historical drawdowns, and sometimes improve ending wealth. For the pooled core plus C1 favorite fades, full Kelly with a 10% cap ends at 3.566× with 19.0% drawdown, versus 5.903× and 50.5% uncapped. A 20% cap retains 5.542× with 34.7% drawdown. With recovered quotes only, all four caps improve that portfolio's full-Kelly ending wealth.

Each bet uses current settled bankroll × min(Kelly multiplier × estimated full-Kelly fraction, cap). The cap includes modeled fees. Smaller calculated bets remain smaller; this is not a strategy that bets the cap on every fight. Each result settles before the next stake. The probability forecasts, price inputs, category memberships and Kelly multipliers are identical between each capped/uncapped pair.

The high-confidence core backs confidence 4–5 at 35–81¢. The broad C1 fade opposes original picks priced at least 50¢; the narrower version uses original asks of 25–70¢. The five-group combination adds C2 backing at 16–36¢, C3 backing at 77 to below 86¢ and C2 fades at approximately 36.644–62¢ to the core/broad-C1-fade portfolio. The two undefeated intervals combine C3–5 backing at 35 to below 40¢ and C1 fades at 25–70¢. All constituent groups are disjoint.

The worst drawdown can move

The maximum drawdown is recomputed over the whole trajectory, not just the two losses behind the earlier 50.5% decline. For the core/favorite-fade full-Kelly strategy, a 20% cap reduces that original two-loss episode to 31.0%, but another earlier episode becomes the maximum at 34.7%. At a 15% cap, the whole-history maximum is 27.75%. For the five-group strategy, a 5% cap leaves a 12.47% maximum decline in a later window.

A per-bet cap is not a maximum-drawdown limit. Two consecutive full losses at 10% each reduce bankroll by 19%; repeated losses can reduce it further. Capping changes both winning and losing stakes, which is why more generous caps do not always produce higher terminal wealth. For example, in the full-price-history core/favorite-fade half-Kelly results, the 15% cap ends slightly above the 20% cap and has a lower maximum drawdown.

What these tests suggest

For a substantial reduction in the observed drawdowns, 10%-capped full Kelly is a candidate worth tracking. It retains 3.566×, 4.079× and 6.509× in the pooled full-price core/favorite-fade, narrow-fade and five-group portfolios, with 19.0%, 19.0% and 21.7% drawdowns. Recovered-price results are much smaller, although the broader portfolios improve on uncapped full Kelly. This is a tradeoff comparison, not a user-selected risk budget.

For preserving more of the aggressive full-history growth, the 20% cap does that. It retains approximately 94%, 89% and 85% of ending wealth for those three portfolios, but historical drawdowns remain around 35%. For a modest adjustment to half Kelly, the 15% cap retains nearly all full-history wealth in the broad core/fade and five-group combinations while removing some of the largest individual positions. Its source-sensitivity results are shown separately.

The 5% cap delivers the smallest drawdowns among these comparisons, at the cost of much more of the favorable full-history growth. No cap is declared universally optimal: the user's four caps are tested as requested, and no preferred drawdown tolerance is assumed. The undefeated intervals still have no historical losing bets to reveal their future drawdown risk, even when capped.
